step-ca Presentation Cheat Sheet

Private PKI, Automated TLS, and Zero-Trust Architecture

Presenter Desk Reference
30-Second Opening Pitch

"Public web security is solved with Let's Encrypt. But inside private company networks (between microservices, databases, and developers), traffic is often unencrypted or relies on brittle 10-year OpenSSL certs that cause catastrophic outages when they expire. step-ca is an open-source, automated private Certificate Authority that issues short-lived, verifiable TLS and SSH certificates on demand."

Slide-by-Slide Timing Guide (7 Mins)
  • Slide 1 (0:00 to 0:45) Introduction: Introduce the problem of internal encryption outages versus public Let's Encrypt.
  • Slide 2 (0:45 to 1:45) The Problem: OpenSSL complexity, unmanaged 5-year cert timebombs, and red browser warnings.
  • Slide 3 (1:45 to 2:45) What is step-ca: Private CA server, ACME server (works with Certbot/Caddy), and SSH Authority.
  • Slide 4 (2:45 to 3:45) Architecture: Clients to Provisioners (SSO/ACME) to Policy/Templates to Signing Engine (KMS/Vault).
  • Slide 5 (3:45 to 4:45) Passive Revocation: 24-hour certs that auto-renew eliminate broken CRLs and slow OCSP responders.
  • Slide 6 (4:45 to 5:15) Code Integration: Walk through server.js showing how Node loads localhost.crt.
  • Slide 7 (5:15 to 6:15) Live Demo: Execute commands (see right box).
  • Slide 8 and 9 (6:15 to 7:00) Business Value: Zero outages, microservice mTLS, and compliance (SOC 2/HIPAA).
60-Second Live Terminal Demo

1. Verify CA Server Health (:9000)

step ca health
# Expected output: ok

2. Issue a New Certificate Instantly

step ca certificate demo.local demo.crt demo.key
# Password: password

3. Inspect the Certificate Specs

step certificate inspect localhost.crt --short
# Show: Issuer (Smallstep Intermediate), Validity (24h)

4. Test Live Node.js HTTPS App (:3000)

curl.exe --ssl-no-revoke --cacert "$env:USERPROFILE\.step\certs\root_ca.crt" https://localhost:3000/
# Expected output: Secure Hello World!
Top 3 Questions Evaluators Ask
Q: "Why not just use regular Let's Encrypt?"
A: Let's Encrypt only validates public domains. It refuses to issue certs for private networks, internal IPs (10.0.0.5), or localhost.
Q: "What happens if a private key is leaked?"
A: Certs only last 24 hours. We revoke it or stop renewals, and the leaked key expires automatically within hours (Passive Revocation).
Q: "How is the root key protected in production?"
A: In production, keys live inside Cloud KMS (AWS/GCP/Azure), Hardware Security Modules (HSM/YubiKey), or HashiCorp Vault, never plaintext on disk.
